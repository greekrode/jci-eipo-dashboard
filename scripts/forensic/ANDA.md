## Thesis

ANDA is a coal contractor that worked for one mine owner for six years, saw its profit fall from Rp66.9bn to Rp1.2bn when that owner's mine plan slipped, and is now raising up to Rp317bn to pay down debt and fund a book of contracts signed this year. The new contracts are real and the customer list is broadening fast. But the price, Rp1.05–1.26tn, assumes those contracts already deliver, when the audited record is a 3.9% margin over the last twelve months, covenants breached at four banks, and a current ratio of 0.70. The insiders who will own three-quarters of the company bought their shares at Rp10 in January 2026, and none of them is locked up.

## How the business works

The company was incorporated in Jakarta in 2014, did nothing operational for four years (its founders never paid in their capital, a gap ratified only in March 2026), and became a mining contractor in August 2018, when current controller Billy Theristine Lim took over as President Director and signed a coal-mining services agreement with PT Budi Gema Gempita (BGG) in Lahat, South Sumatra. It removes overburden, digs and hauls coal and maintains haul roads, billed per bank cubic metre, per tonne or per tonne-kilometre, with a fleet of 33 excavators, 112 trucks and 41 support units that is largely financed or pledged. It also rents equipment with operators. Headcount is 762, almost all at the sites.

BGG was 100% of revenue in FY2023 and FY2024 and 94% in FY2025. The agreement runs "seven years from signing", which is August 2025 if counted from 2018 or about 2029 if counted from the January 2022 addendum; the prospectus gives neither an expiry date nor a renewal, and its stated contract value (Rp87.7bn) is a quarter of one year's BGG billings. In the first half of 2026 BGG revenue roughly halved year on year to Rp104.5bn, and its share fell to 52% as new work arrived: a Petrosea subcontract at PT Daya Bumindo Karunia's Murung Raya concession (Rp991.7bn to December 2029, 15% of revenue), production support for PT Satria Bahana Sarana at Tanjung Enim (Rp208.9bn to 2029, 13%), nickel ore-getting for PT Position in Halmahera Timur, an exclusive contract with PT Intan Bumi Persada to 2030, and five-year contracts with two related parties, PT Arkara Prathama Energi in Kutai Timur and PT Bukit Bara Alam in Lahat. None of these has a full year in the audited accounts, and the prospectus discloses no production volumes, utilisation or historical rates.

## The offering & ownership

Up to 1,676,000,000 new shares, 100% primary, at Rp158–189 (par Rp10) for 25.10% of a 6,676,000,000-share company: Rp264.8–316.8bn gross. Every seven new shares carry one free Series I warrant (up to 239,429,000), exercisable from June 2027 to December 2028 at Rp695, which is 3.7 to 4.4 times the offer price; the draft gives three different warrant counts and never explains the exercise price. PT Henan Putihrai Sekuritas is sole lead on full commitment, with the syndicate, fees and issuance costs still blank. The listing is on the Development Board.

Billy Theristine Lim is the designated sole controller and beneficial owner: 30% direct before the IPO (22.47% after) and about 33% effective through stakes in two holding companies. Her partner Ivan Victor owns 51% of PT Bumi Artha Caraka and 99% of PT Arkara Danatama Nusantara, which hold 37.44% between them after the IPO, yet he is not described as a controller. Those two vehicles were incorporated in December 2024 and March 2025 with Rp2.5bn of capital each and each paid Rp12.5bn into ANDA in January 2026, at Rp10 a share, alongside PT Bhumi Caraka Persada (controlled by commissioner Freddy Hartono) and Billy herself: Rp39.5bn in all, with no share premium and no disclosed source of funds. That payment landed about four weeks before the six-month look-back from the August 2026 filing, so POJK 25/2017 locks up no one. All 5.0bn pre-IPO shares, 74.9% of the company, can be sold from listing day; the only undertaking is Billy's promise not to give up control for twelve months.

## Governance & related parties

The board was rebuilt for the IPO. The audit committee, internal audit unit and corporate secretary were all created on 3 March 2026; two of five commissioners are independent and there is no independent director. The President Director, Gahari Christine, is the controller's younger sibling. The head of internal audit is concurrently GM Finance of the "Arkara Group" of companies around the controllers.

The counterparties are the same people. PT Arkara Prathama Energi, now a customer to 2030, was run by Ivan Victor until 2026, counts Billy among its commissioners, and gives a corporate and cash-deficit guarantee on ANDA's Rp203bn Mandiri loan. PT Bukit Bara Alam, a customer to 2031, is run by commissioner Freddy Hartono, and in the first half of 2026 it paid ANDA Rp5.17bn to cancel an equipment rental. PT Andalan Bhumi Nusantara, under common control, rents equipment in both directions. Billy, Ivan and Freddy pledge shares of their other companies to Mandiri, Billy and two others give personal guarantees, and Billy lent the company Rp64bn interest-free, all repaid in the same half-year the new equity arrived. Counsel declines to opine on whether the affiliate contracts are at arm's length, and a September 2026 energy-ministry decree now requires ministerial approval for new affiliate mining-service contracts. The legal opinion is otherwise clean on litigation, tax and labour; spatial-use approvals are pending at three sites and the mining-services licence expires in December 2027.

## Reading the financials

Revenue fell from Rp517.0bn (FY2023) to Rp415.4bn and then Rp368.8bn, and first-half 2026 revenue was down 8.7% to Rp200.1bn. FY2023 was the only year with a real margin: Rp66.9bn net, 12.9%. In FY2024 BGG's annual mine plan was approved only in late January, heavy rain caused a landslide and washed out a bridge, and fuel climbed to 32% of revenue, while depreciation kept rising on Rp218bn of equipment bought that year; net profit fell 98% to Rp1.2bn. FY2025 net profit of Rp5.1bn includes a Rp5.26bn insurance claim. First-half 2026 net profit of Rp11.8bn includes the Rp5.17bn Bukit Bara Alam compensation, about a third of pre-tax profit, and the gross-margin recovery to 25.9% partly reflects principals now supplying the fuel. Over the twelve months to June 2026 the company earned Rp13.7bn on Rp349.8bn of revenue.

Operating cash flow has been positive every period (Rp116bn in FY2025), and EBITDA is around Rp160bn a year. The problem is what sits above it. Depreciation alone was Rp104.5bn in FY2025 (28% of revenue), interest-bearing debt is Rp482bn (2.04x equity, about 3x EBITDA), interest cover was 1.17x and debt-service cover 0.54x. At 30 June 2026 the company was in breach of covenants at Mandiri, BCA, BRI and BNI. Waivers tied to the IPO cover the first three, one of them dated October 2026, and Rp212.7bn of bank debt that was current at December 2025 now sits as non-current. BNI has given no waiver and tests on 31 December 2026. Cash was Rp12.5bn against Rp209.3bn of payments due within a year, and 45% of trade payables were more than 90 days overdue. Before the IPO the company sold and leased back equipment it already owned at 14–15%, and borrowed Rp15bn from another company and Rp13bn interest-free from an individual to fill the gap. Equity reached Rp236.3bn in June 2026 only because of the Rp39.5bn injection.

## Valuation

At Rp158–189 the company is worth Rp1,054.8–1,261.8bn (Rp1.09–1.31tn if every warrant is exercised). That is 205–245x FY2025 earnings, 77–92x the twelve months to June 2026, and 45–54x annualised first-half 2026 profit, which includes the affiliate compensation. Only against FY2023, the year before the collapse, does it look ordinary at 16–19x. On assets the picture is milder: 4.5–5.3x book before the money, 2.15–2.33x after it, about 3x sales, and roughly 8–9x trailing EV/EBITDA once net debt and the proceeds are counted. Return on equity was 2.8% in FY2025 and falls to about 1–3% on the enlarged equity. The prospectus offers no peer table or valuation basis; the price is whatever bookbuilding bears. Buying here means paying for the 2026 contract book before it has produced a year of audited results.

## Bottom line

There is a real business here: an eight-year operator with a heavy fleet, positive operating cash flow, and a customer list that went from one name to seven in a year, including Petrosea and a Bukit Asam subsidiary. The IPO, if fully sold at the top of the range, would repay Rp107bn of expensive debt and cut total liabilities from 2.6x equity to about 1x. Against that: one customer whose contract has no stated end date and whose billings halved this year, covenant breaches at four banks, affiliates on both sides of the new contracts, thin earnings padded by an affiliate's compensation payment and an insurance claim, and insiders sitting on 15–19x their January entry price with nothing stopping them from selling. The underwriter is leading alone for the first time after five co-led deals, four of which fell 81–87% from their early highs, and press reports say OJK fined it in October 2026 over AML controls in the 2023 CUAN allocation. Wait for audited full-year 2026 results showing the new contracts deliver margin and the BNI covenant clears. At this price, with no lock-up and a 25% float, an early sell-off by insiders is as likely as a pop.
